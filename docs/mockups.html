Photographs of the running build

The app, screen by screen

Every frame below is a capture of the real app, taken by mobile/scripts/capture-screenshots.mjs: the web build is exported, every Supabase request is answered from a fixture file, and the screens are photographed in a phone-sized browser. The layout, the type and the wording are whatever the components currently render — only the league behind them is invented.

This page used to draw each screen by hand so the annotations had something to point at. It also claimed that every colour and type size on it came from mobile/lib/theme.ts, and the first time the theme changed that stopped being true and nobody noticed — a drawing cannot fail. The captions are the part worth keeping, so they are now attached to the photographs instead. Regenerating the screenshots updates this page too.

Light is the default; dark is built separately rather than inverted, and the app follows the system setting. Where a screen is shown twice, the second is the same screen on a phone set to dark. Screens taller than the frame are cut off at the bottom — that is where the app scrolls.

Lead and bone

Both palettes come from an indoor 10 m range rather than from a component library. Amber is the only accent and it is spent on one thing: whatever is live and waiting for you. Won and lost are a desaturated olive and brick — they report rather than shout. These are the values in mobile/lib/theme.ts; this page is set in them, so if they stop working it stops looking right.

Decisions

What moved, and why. Information, not ornament.

DecisionWhy
The plate never changes theme A score printed on a target card is pitch on bone in a bright hall and in a dark one, because that is what the object is. Everything else flips; the plate does not.
Only what is unresolved gets a plate Deliberate scarcity. A screen of decided series should not shout as loudly as the series being shot tonight, so settled ones collapse to two ruled lines and four now fit where two did.
A shutter, not a grey placeholder The blind reveal is the product. "Hidden until Stefan submits" describes it; a sealed plate says a number exists and is not yours to see yet, which is a stronger thing to look at.
Amber is the only accent One saturated thing per screen, and it is always the action you can take right now. "Your turn" and "closing soon" are the same signal as the lane light, so warning and accent are the same value.
Nearly square corners (2–5 pt) The old 18 pt radius was what made every screen read as a stack of app cards. Equipment and printed cards are cut, not rounded.
Cards separate with a hairline A drop shadow is the one thing that never happens on card stock, and it was doing work that a rule does better and quieter.
Three faces, three jobs Archivo for headings and scores, Instrument Sans for running text, IBM Plex Mono for anything read as a value rather than as a sentence. Before this there was no typeface at all, which is the single biggest reason the app read as generic.
The ten-shot string Not a score, but the shape of how it was arrived at — the one thing this league holds that nobody else does. Drawn whenever a range could export submissions.shots, and absent without complaint when it could not.
Tables where there are tables Rankings gets a header row and two weights of rule, which is what a printed results sheet does and what a shooter already knows how to read.
No initials tiles "MF" in a coloured square stood in for an identity nobody has uploaded. The club and country say more, and give a captain something to point at.
52 pt buttons, sizes unchanged Cold hands, dim hall, shooting glasses, shooters who skew older. The type scale deliberately survived the repaint intact: this was a change of voice, not of size.
No coloured header bar It cost 80 points of height and carried no information. Screens open with a large title on the content ground.
Inner tens only for pistol ISSF breaks a tied full-ring score on inner tens. Decimal scores break their own ties, so rifle shooters type one number.

01 — Without an account

Seasons, tables and finished matches are readable by anyone; a match still being shot is not. This is the only page a shooter ever arrives at cold, from a club chat group or a link a captain sent round.

Enforced below

The split between these pages and the rest is in the database, not the client. Spectators read views that run with the owner's rights and expose settled results only; the tables underneath are not granted to anon at all. A match in progress is exactly what the blind reveal protects, so it appears on no public surface.

02 — Getting in

Signing up records an age and a consent you have to reach for. A shooter enters a season themselves; a club is entered by one of its officials.

03 — Competing

The first card is the answer to "I am at a range right now": shoot a series with no opponent in sight, and be compared with whoever reports one next.

Reads as a page

Decided series collapse to two ruled lines each, with only the winning number in full ink. That is what lets four series fit where two did, and it is why the eye lands on the live one: it is the only thing on the screen carrying weight.

04 — Reporting and checking

Two numbers at most, and a photo of the display. Deliberately no OCR: typing one number is not worth automating, and the verification is done by the opponent after the reveal — motivated, and more reliable than a model reading a photograph of a monitor.

05 — The table and the shooter

Two records that answer different questions. Your shooting is everything you reported; your rating is only what somebody checked.

06 — Settling a disagreement

When two shooters cannot agree, a referee gets both photos and four ways to end it. Peer confirmation works while everyone knows each other; this is what has to exist before that stops being true.

These frames are regenerated by cd mobile && npm run screenshots, which needs playwright — deliberately not a dependency of the app, since it is only used to take the pictures. The same run writes docs/screenshots/, which the README embeds.

What is invented is the league: the shooters, clubs and results come from mobile/scripts/fixtures.mjs, and the ten-shot strings there add up to the totals they sit under, because the database enforces exactly that cross-check when a range supplies individual shots.

The design tokens are in mobile/lib/theme.ts and the components that use them in mobile/components/ui.tsx.
